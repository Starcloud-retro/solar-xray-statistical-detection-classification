Our detector says “something unusual happened.” NOAA provides an independent flare record to compare against.

Matching uses interval overlap or sufficiently close peak timing. That lets us measure detection performance without pretending the detector’s own output is ground truth.

PEAK TIMING

Peak timing is useful, but not perfect

This particular matched event has a 9-minute absolute peak difference. Across matched events the median absolute peak error is 0 minutes and the mean is about 1.76 minutes.

AGGREGATE VIEW

115 detections do not become 115 confirmed flares

The frozen alignment produced 32 matched detections, 80 unmatched detections, and 3 unmatched NOAA events.

METRICS

Precision and recall answer different questions

Precision = 0.2857: among evaluated detections, how often was there a NOAA match? Recall = 0.9143: among NOAA events, how many did the detector recover? F1 = 0.4354.

CAUTION

Unmatched does not automatically mean “physically false”

Some unmatched detections may be sub-B activity or noise. The alignment tells us whether NOAA recorded a matching event, not whether every unmatched signal is physically meaningless.
