04 · DEFINE NORMAL

The Sun does not have one fixed background. So “normal” must be estimated from the recent past.

We use the previous 30 minutes of log10 X-ray flux to estimate local center and spread. The current minute is excluded from its own reference window.

STEP 1

Look only backward

The shaded region is the previous 30 minutes. The current point sits outside that window, so it cannot pull its own reference upward.

STEP 2

Find the center

Mean asks for the arithmetic center. Median asks for the middle value. Both describe local level, but the median is less sensitive to an extreme point.

STEP 3

Measure the spread

SD summarizes typical deviation from the mean. MAD uses absolute deviations from the median, so it is more robust to large spikes.

STEP 4

Why multiply MAD by 1.4826?

For normally distributed data, raw MAD is about 0.67449σ. Dividing by 0.67449 is approximately the same as multiplying by 1.4826.

scaled MAD = 1.4826 × raw MAD

STEP 5

Now the current minute can be judged fairly

At 19:04 UTC the real point is already several local spread units above its recent background. That creates the bridge from “normal” to anomaly scoring.
