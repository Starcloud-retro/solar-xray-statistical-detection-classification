03 · PREPARE DECISION

How do we treat missing minutes without pretending they were measured?

This component compares two real cases from the cleaned GOES X-ray series. In the first, a single missing minute is short enough to be interpolated. In the second, a much longer gap stays missing and creates a break between continuous signal segments.

RULE A Short gaps ≤ 3 minutes

Interpolate carefully, then mark the reconstructed rows as imputed rather than observed.

RULE B Long gaps > 3 minutes

Preserve as missing, do not bridge the break, and restart the later continuous segment.

REAL EXAMPLE A · 6 SEP 2026

One missing minute inside an otherwise continuous signal

RAW GAP

19:34 UTC has no direct raw measurement. The surrounding minutes remain observed.

STEP 1

See the raw short gap

One minute is missing inside an otherwise continuous section of the signal.

STEP 2

Apply the short-gap rule

Because the gap length is only one minute, it satisfies the ≤ 3 minute interpolation rule.

STEP 3

Reconstruct — but mark it honestly

The cleaned value is inserted and flagged as imputed so later analysis can distinguish it from a true observation.

STEP 4

Now compare a long gap

A six-minute absence is too long to reconstruct safely.

STEP 5

Preserve the break

The cleaned series stays broken across the long gap. No false line is drawn between the two sides.

STEP 6

Start a new usable segment

Rows after the long gap belong to a later continuous segment for downstream calculations.
